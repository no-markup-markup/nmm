¶ 1.1  This is a no-markup-markup document, sporting the following
       cool features:

       (1)   E̲m̲p̲h̲a̲s̲i̲z̲e̲d̲ ̲t̲e̲x̲t̲ ̲s̲p̲a̲n̲n̲i̲n̲g̲ ̲m̲u̲l̲t̲i̲p̲l̲e̲ ̲l̲i̲n̲e̲s̲ ̲i̲n̲ ̲t̲h̲e̲ ̲s̲o̲u̲r̲c̲e̲
             ̲c̲o̲d̲e̲ ̲w̲i̲t̲h̲o̲u̲t̲ ̲b̲r̲e̲a̲k̲i̲n̲g̲ ̲a̲ ̲s̲w̲e̲a̲t̲.

       (2)   Bullet lists:

             ─     Here is one bullet item.

             ─     Here is another one.

       (3)   Numbered lists with labels that are either

             (a)   automatic, or

             (*)   custom.

             Furthermore, each item can be n̲a̲m̲e̲d̲ and r̲e̲f̲e̲r̲r̲e̲d̲ ̲t̲o̲:

             (b)   The named item can be referred to anywhere in the
                   document.

             (c)   When referring to (b) above in a local manner
                   (which is default), any common initial path
                   shared between the location of the referring
                   expression and the location of its referent will
                   be omitted. The same applies with respect to (†)
                   below.

             (†)   Of course, items with custom labels can also be
                   named!

       Due to the feature mentioned in (3)(c), a local reference to
       (3)(b) or (3)(†) occurring elsewhere may appear differently.
       You can also refer to (3)(b) and (3)(†) globally, rendering
       their full path.

       (4)   Displayed lines of text suitable for mathematical
             equations, which are either

             (a)   automatically labeled (and named),
             (+)   custom labeled (and named), or,
                   unlike (a) above, unlabeled.

       Displayed lines can also be used for drawing tables and such:

                 |    0    |      1      |    2      | . . .
             ───────────────────────────────────────────────
             f₀  | f₀(0) + 1 | f₀(1)     | f₀(2)     |
             f₁  | f₁(0)     | f₁(1) + 1 | f₁(2)     |
             f₂  | f₂(0)     | f₂(1)     | f₂(2) + 1 |
             .                                         .
             .                                           .
             .                                             .

       This is what a reference to (4)(a) looks like.

¶ 2.3  DEFINITION  We say that Γ ⊨ φ just in case every model of Γ
       is a model of φ.


¶ 2.4  DEFINITION (Provability)  We say that Γ ⊢ φ just in case
       there is a proof from Γ to φ.


¶ 2.5  FACT  For every sentence φ and set of sentences Γ, if Γ ⊨ φ
       then Γ ⊢ φ.
